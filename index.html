CELEBRATING THE LIFE OF OUR BELOVED FATHER

PA JOSEPH AJIBADE OYAKALE OYATOLA

1931 – 24th September 2026

Forever loved. Forever remembered. Forever cherished.

“Baba, you came, you fought, and indeed, you conquered.”

With profound gratitude to God, we celebrate the remarkable life and legacy of our beloved father, grandfather, great-grandfather, mentor and friend, Pa Joseph Ajibade Oyakale Oyatola.

Baba lived a long, purposeful and fulfilled life. He passed away peacefully on 24th September 2026, at the age of 95.

His Early Years and Journey to Ghana

Baba was born in 1931 at Sese’s Compound, Inisa, Odo Otin Local Government Area, Osun State, Nigeria, to his late parents, Mr Oyatola and Mrs Irele. His mother hailed from Ile Oree, Inisa.

As a young man, Baba embarked on his first international journey to the Gold Coast, now Ghana, in pursuit of a better life and business opportunities.

While in Ghana, he engaged in various means of livelihood, including apprenticeship, the sale of clothing materials and driving trucks. He stayed with his late uncles, Pa Raphael Oyadiji and Baba Adeyefa, who supported him during his early years there.

Baba was resourceful and determined. During his stay in Ghana, he had the opportunity to attend a form of home schooling, through which he learned to read and write in Yoruba and acquired some knowledge of English.

He lived in places including Kumasi, Accra, Kotopo and Asikuma, and became fluent in Ana, one of the Ghanaian languages. His years in Ghana helped shape the hardworking, disciplined and resourceful man he later became.

Marriage and Family

In 1955, Baba returned to Nigeria and married his beloved wife, Maria Oladoyinbo Abeke, from Oke Ayan Compound in Inisa.

After their marriage, Baba and Mama Maria returned to Ghana, where they continued their life together before eventually returning permanently to Nigeria following the death of his eldest brother, Pa Oyakale Tardius, of blessed memory.

In keeping with the Yoruba tradition of the time, Baba took responsibility for the family of his late brother, including his brother’s wife, Mama Theresa Oyakale Abeni, of blessed memory. This demonstrated one of Baba’s defining qualities: his deep sense of family responsibility.

A Life of Hard Work and Farming

Following the death of his brother in 1966, Baba moved to Alabameta, a village near Ile-Ife, Osun State, where he took over the farm left by his late brother. There, he spent approximately 40 years as a farmer.

Baba was exceptionally hardworking. He was never afraid of work and could perform virtually every task required on the farm. He often worked from morning until evening, sometimes without stopping to eat.

He worked with his hands, endured difficult circumstances and provided for his family through honest labour. For Baba, there was dignity in work. He taught us that whatever our circumstances may be, we must work hard, remain responsible and never give up.

After about four decades in Alabameta, Baba relocated with his wife to Inisa as he grew older, following the collective decision of his children.

A Father Who Valued Education

One of Baba’s greatest legacies was his commitment to the education of his children. Although he worked extremely hard as a farmer to provide for his family, he understood the importance of education and did everything within his ability to ensure that his children went to school. He often reminded us that farming was hard work and that schooling offered a better path to opportunity.

He made enormous sacrifices for his children. Today, the fruits of those sacrifices are evident. Among his children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren are pastors, CEOs, school proprietors, business tycoons, lawyers, doctors, accountants, engineers, bankers and professionals in various fields.

Baba may not have had the opportunity to receive extensive formal education himself, but he understood the transformative power of education. He invested in the lives of his children, and generations after him continue to benefit from that investment.

A Man of Character

Baba was a man of strong character. He was:

  • Hardworking and industrious
  • Disciplined and prudent
  • Truthful and responsible
  • Caring and brave
  • Family-oriented

He was a disciplinarian who believed strongly in doing what was right. He was also a prudent man who understood the value of resources and taught his family not to waste what they had.

Baba was truly a “jack of all trades and master of many.” From farming to trading, driving and other practical activities, he was always willing to learn, work and provide for his family.

His Faith

Baba was a devoted Christian and communicant until his death. While working on the farm, he often sang this song:

Igbeyin ni mo ro Ti mo fi gba Jesu gbo Baba mimo olore Jeki igbemi dara Jeki igbemi dara Ma se jeki ya jemi Adura ti mo gba Jesu bami mu se Oba mimo olore Jeki igbemi dara

His faith was an important part of his life, and he remained committed to the Christian way of life. Baba had the privilege of confessing Jesus as his personal Lord and Saviour. He died in the Lord.

Good Night, Baba

You taught us many lessons about life. You showed us that a person does not need to be born into wealth to leave a rich legacy.

You showed us that hard work, discipline, sacrifice, faith and perseverance can transform a family for generations.

Your hands may now be still, but the fruits of your labour continue to speak. Your voice may no longer be heard, but your teachings remain with us. Your physical presence may be gone, but your legacy lives on in your children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

We thank God for the gift of your life.
We thank God for the years He gave you.
We thank God for the family you raised.
We thank God for the legacy you left behind.

Rest peacefully, our beloved Baba.

You lived a long and fulfilled life.
You served your family.
You served your generation.
You left a legacy.

And we shall continue to honour your memory.
